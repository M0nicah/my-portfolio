Analytical Thinking in Practice

Short investigations and micro-analyses that show how I approach real-world data questions — the reasoning, not just the result.

Fintech · FX
Why do mobile money platforms lose margin during high-volatility FX sessions?
When USD/KES rates move sharply intraday, fixed-spread pricing creates asymmetric risk — the platform buys at a worse rate than customers are quoted. By mapping transaction timestamps against FX tick data, I identified that ~30% of cross-border volume clustered in the 90-minute window after Nairobi market open — the highest-volatility period.
Finding ~30%
of cross-border volume occurs during peak FX volatility — a dynamic spread mechanism would protect margins without affecting most users.
Retail · Profitability
Can a product generate high revenue but negative profit? How do you catch it?
Aggregate dashboards flatten margins — a product with 40% of sales but thin or negative margins looks fine in a revenue summary. The fix is building a profitability view alongside revenue: SUM(profit)/SUM(sales) segmented by sub-category, with conditional formatting to flag anything below a threshold.
Finding
In the Superstore dataset, the "Tables" sub-category had high sales rank but was one of the worst profit contributors — invisible without a margin-first view.
Income · Seasonality
How can a freelancer use data to smooth out income volatility?
Most freelancers track earnings reactively — they know Q1 was slow but don't know why or by how much. By indexing monthly earnings to the annual mean and computing rolling 3-month averages, clear seasonal patterns emerge that can inform pricing, pipeline management, and savings strategy.
Finding −25%
Q1 earnings consistently fell ~25% below annual average — predictable enough to plan against with a 2-month cash buffer and pre-booked Q4 retainers.
SQL · Technique
When should you use a window function instead of a GROUP BY?
GROUP BY collapses rows — you lose access to row-level context. Window functions let you compute aggregates alongside the original detail. For revenue analysis: running totals, rank within group, or period-over-period % change are all cleaner as window functions. Example: LAG(revenue,1) OVER (PARTITION BY region ORDER BY month).
Rule of Thumb
If you need aggregation and row detail together — reach for a window function. If you only need the summary — GROUP BY is cleaner and faster.
Reconciliation · Ops
What does 100% reconciliation accuracy actually require operationally?
Daily reconciliation between M-Pesa/Airtel statements and internal ledgers isn't just a matching exercise — it's a data pipeline. You need consistent field mapping, handling of pending/reversed transactions, duplicate detection, and a clear escalation path for discrepancies. Automating the delta report makes exceptions visible immediately rather than accumulating.
Practice
Discrepancies resolved within 24 hours at DCASH Africa by building a daily SQL exception report that flagged any transaction where provider record ≠ internal balance update.
KPI Design
How do you build a KPI framework that actually connects to business goals?
Most dashboards measure activity, not outcomes. A proper KPI framework starts with a business goal, then works backwards: what leading indicators predict that outcome? What operational metrics drive those indicators? The result is a metrics tree — not a list of numbers, but a causal chain.
Framework
Goal → Outcome metric → Leading indicator → Operational metric → Data source. Used this structure in both the EduMap dashboard and client BI engagements.
