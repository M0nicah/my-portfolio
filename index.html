Data Bites

Analytical Thinking in Practice

Short write-ups on real data problems. The focus is on how I think through a question, not just what the answer was.

Fintech · FX
Why do mobile money platforms lose margin during high-volatility FX sessions?
When the USD/KES rate swings intraday, a fixed spread means the platform is selling at a rate it locked in before the move. Matching transaction timestamps to FX tick data showed that around 30% of cross-border volume happened in the 90 minutes after the Nairobi market opened, which is typically the most volatile window.
Finding ~30%
of cross-border volume falls in the peak volatility window. Adjusting the spread during that window would protect margins for the majority of transfers.
Retail · Profitability
Can a product generate high revenue but negative profit? How do you catch it?
A revenue-only view hides margin problems. A product can move a lot of units but still cost the business money if the margin is negative. Adding a profit rate column (SUM(profit)/SUM(sales)) broken out by sub-category, with a threshold flag, makes these cases easy to spot.
Finding
In the Superstore dataset, Tables ranked high on sales but were actually losing money. It only showed up once I looked at profit rate per category rather than total revenue.
Income · Seasonality
How can a freelancer use data to smooth out income volatility?
Most freelancers know when a bad month happened but have no idea whether it was unusual or part of a pattern. Indexing monthly earnings against the annual average and running 3-month rolling averages makes the seasonal shape visible and repeatable.
Finding -25%
Q1 consistently came in about 25% below the annual average. That is a predictable enough pattern to plan around: build a cash buffer in Q4 and lock in retainer work before January.
SQL · Technique
When should you use a window function instead of a GROUP BY?
GROUP BY collapses your rows into a summary. Once you group, you lose the row-level detail. Window functions let you calculate things like running totals, ranks, or month-on-month change while keeping every row intact. Example: LAG(revenue,1) OVER (PARTITION BY region ORDER BY month).
Rule of Thumb
If you need both the aggregate and the original rows in the same result set, use a window function. If you just need the summary, GROUP BY is simpler and usually faster.
Reconciliation · Ops
What does 100% reconciliation accuracy actually require operationally?
Reconciliation is more than matching numbers. You need consistent field mapping, logic for pending and reversed transactions, duplicate detection, and a clear process for anything that does not match. The key is automating a daily exceptions report so mismatches are visible the same day, not after they stack up.
Practice
At DCASH Africa, a daily SQL report flagged any transaction where the provider record did not match the internal ledger. Mismatches were investigated and resolved within 24 hours.
KPI Design
How do you build a KPI framework that actually connects to business goals?
Most dashboards count things without asking whether those things matter. A useful KPI structure starts from the business goal and works backwards: what would tell us we are on track? What day-to-day metric feeds that? Where does the data come from? That way every number on the dashboard has a clear reason to be there.
Structure
Goal, then outcome metric, then leading indicator, then operational metric, then data source. I used this structure on the EduMap dashboard and in client engagements.
